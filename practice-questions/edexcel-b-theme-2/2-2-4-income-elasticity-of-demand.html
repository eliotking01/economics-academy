2.2.4 Income Elasticity of Demand — Practice Questions

Seven original multiple-choice questions on income elasticity of demand: calculating and interpreting YED, luxuries, necessities and inferior goods, the factors that affect YED, and why it matters to firms. Two are calculations.

7 questions Edexcel B A-Level Multiple choice Model answers included

Not read the notes yet? Start with the 2.2.4 Income Elasticity of Demand revision notes.

7 questions in this set

  1. 1. Average real incomes fall by 4% in a recession. Over the same period a retailer's monthly sales of hot tubs fall from 250 to 220, with its prices unchanged. Measuring each percentage change against its original value, and to one decimal place, the income elasticity of demand for its hot tubs is

    Calculation

    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: D (+3.0). % change in quantity demanded = (220 − 250) ÷ 250 × 100 = −12%.
    % change in real income = −4%.
    YED = −12 ÷ −4 = +3.0.
    Two negatives make a positive: sales and income moved in the same direction, so hot tubs are a normal good. YED above 1 makes them a luxury: sales fell three times as fast as income.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • A — The size is right but the sign is wrong. Sales fell, but so did income, and dividing a negative by a negative gives a positive. Keep the sign of YED: a negative value would wrongly make hot tubs an inferior good.
    • B — Two errors at once: the formula is inverted (4 ÷ 12) and the sign is wrong. The % change in quantity demanded goes on top, and a fall in sales alongside a fall in income gives a positive YED.
    • C — The formula has been inverted: 4 ÷ 12 = 0.3, to one decimal place. The % change in quantity demanded goes on top. Read this way, hot tubs would wrongly look like a necessity.

  2. 2. A launderette chain estimates that the income elasticity of demand for its service is −0.8. All other things being equal, this means that

    Definition in context

    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: B (A 10% rise in real incomes cuts the number of washes by about 8%.). % change in quantity demanded = YED × % change in real income = −0.8 × +10% = −8%.
    The negative sign means the launderette's service is an inferior good: as people earn more, they buy their own washing machines and use launderettes less.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • A — The formula has been inverted: 10 ÷ 0.8 = 12.5%. A YED of size 0.8 means quantity changes by less than income, not more.
    • C — The minus sign has been dropped, as if this were PED. With YED the sign is the answer: −0.8 means demand moves against income.
    • D — This describes price elasticity of demand. YED measures the response of quantity demanded to a change in real income, not to the product's own price.

  3. 3. A department store estimates the income elasticity of demand for four of its product ranges, shown in Table 1. Real incomes are forecast to fall by 2%.
    Using Table 1, the range whose sales are forecast to change by the smallest percentage, up or down, is

    Data interpretation

    Table 1: Income elasticity of demand for four product ranges
    Product range YED
    Range 1 +2.2
    Range 2 +1.0
    Range 3 +0.1
    Range 4 −0.6
    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: C (Range 3.). % change in sales = YED × % change in real income. With real incomes down 2%:
    Range 1: +2.2 × −2 = −4.4%.
    Range 2: +1.0 × −2 = −2.0%.
    Range 3: +0.1 × −2 = −0.2%.
    Range 4: −0.6 × −2 = +1.2%.
    Range 3 is a necessity with a YED close to zero, so its sales barely move whatever happens to income.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • A — Range 1 is a luxury, and its sales change the most: a 4.4% fall. Customers cut back on luxuries first when incomes fall.
    • B — A YED of +1.0 does not mean sales are unaffected. It means they change by the same percentage as income, here a 2% fall, which is ten times Range 3's change.
    • D — This is the trap. Range 4 is an inferior good, so its sales rise when incomes fall, but they rise by 1.2%. That is a bigger change than Range 3's 0.2% fall. The question asks for the smallest change, up or down.

  4. 4. A travel firm sells luxury safari holidays, with an income elasticity of demand of +2.5, and budget coach holidays in the UK, with an income elasticity of demand of −0.5. Real incomes are forecast to fall for the next two years. The best way for the firm to plan its holiday places is to

    Applied reasoning

    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: D (Cut safari places and add coach-holiday places.). Safaris are a luxury (YED above 1): real incomes fall → customers cut back on luxuries first → safari bookings fall sharply.
    Coach holidays are an inferior good (YED below 0): real incomes fall → customers trade down from dearer trips → coach-holiday bookings rise.
    So the firm should move capacity from safaris to coach holidays, much as a car dealer expecting a downturn orders fewer new cars and more used ones.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • A — This is the plan for a boom, when rising incomes lift demand for luxuries and cut it for inferior goods. Here incomes are forecast to fall.
    • B — Demand for a normal good falls with incomes, but the coach holidays have a negative YED. Demand for them rises when incomes fall.
    • C — This gets both the wrong way round. The negative YED means coach-holiday demand rises in a downturn, and safaris, with YED +2.5, are the bookings most likely to fall.

  5. 5. Estimates of the income elasticity of demand for new cars are +2.5 in a low-income country and +0.4 in a high-income country. The best explanation for the difference is that, in the high-income country, cars

    Applied reasoning

    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: A (Are a necessity for most households, not a luxury.). YED depends on the customer's income level. The same product can be a luxury for low-income households and a necessity for high-income ones. In the low-income country, YED of +2.5 shows a luxury: households buy cars as they get richer. In the high-income country most households already have one, so a rise in income changes car buying only a little: YED of +0.4 shows a necessity.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • B — An inferior good has a negative YED. At +0.4, demand for cars still rises with income, just by less, so cars are a normal good in both countries.
    • C — Close substitutes matter mainly for PED. For YED, alternatives matter when people swap to something better as they earn more, which makes a good inferior. At +0.4, demand for cars still rises with income. The difference comes from income level: most households in the richer country already own a car.
    • D — Share of income is also a factor in PED, not YED. And a car takes up a smaller share of a typical income in a richer country, not a larger one.

  6. 6. A furniture firm sells only hand-made sofas, with an income elasticity of demand of +2.8. It plans to add a range of budget flat-pack sofas, with an income elasticity of demand of −0.4. The main benefit to the firm is most likely that

    Applied reasoning

    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: D (Its total sales will be steadier over the cycle.). The two ranges respond to income in opposite directions. In a boom, sales of hand-made sofas rise sharply while sales of the budget range fall. In a recession, hand-made sofa sales fall sharply while the budget range gains as shoppers trade down. Selling both is a product portfolio: when one sells less the other sells more, so total sales are steadier across the economic cycle.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • A — YED says nothing about what customers will pay for a product. Adding a budget range does not make demand for the hand-made sofas any more price inelastic.
    • B — This is the wrong way round. A negative YED means the budget range sells less when incomes rise, and more when they fall.
    • C — Hand-made sofa sales already rise fast in a boom, because their YED is +2.8. Adding a separate budget range does not change that.

  7. 7. Over a year, money incomes rise by 7% and prices by 3%, so real incomes rise by 4%. A garden-design firm's sales rise from 150 makeovers to 159 over the same year, with its prices unchanged. To one decimal place, the income elasticity of demand for its makeovers is

    Calculation

    Select one answer

    Show model answer

    Answer: C (+1.5). % change in quantity demanded = (159 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 = +6%.
    YED measures the response to real income, which rose by 7% − 3% = 4%.
    YED = +6 ÷ +4 = +1.5.
    Above 1, so the makeovers are a luxury: sales grew faster than real income.

    Why the other options are wrong

    • A — The formula has been inverted: 4 ÷ 6 = 0.67, or 0.7 to one decimal place. The % change in quantity demanded goes on top.
    • B — This divides by the 7% rise in money income: 6 ÷ 7 = 0.86. YED uses real income, because only a rise in real income lets people buy more. Using money income here would wrongly make the makeovers look like a necessity.
    • D — This divides by the 3% rise in prices: 6 ÷ 3 = 2.0. That is the inflation rate, not the change in income at all.
