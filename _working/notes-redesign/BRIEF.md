## What you are redesigning, and why

The pages are generated: `build_notes_pages.py` takes each slice from `notes-data/topics/` and splices chrome around it at build time — the established pattern is that the slices are never written to and every added block lives in the generator (`notes_extras.apply_all()`, `with_topic_nav()`, `with_webp_pictures()`). The visual design is the 2025 "textbook" sheet: a white card with a drop shadow, an `h1` with a teal underline, gradient bars on every `h2`, dashed rules on `h3`, five callout boxes with floating pill labels (SPECIFICATION, EXAM TIP, WORKED EXAMPLE, EVALUATION, and APPLICATION — unused on any page) plus the pink `key-definition` and green `content-example` inline chips, gradient table headers, Merriweather body at 1.05em with Source Sans Pro headings. Four passes this month bolted good things onto it (contents list, sub-label, byline, tail, prev/next, self-hosted fonts, print, reduced motion) without ever revisiting the base, and `OWNER-TODO.md` records that nobody has yet looked at a finished topic page in a browser. The sheet itself carries duplicated rule blocks, nested media queries and `!important`s. None of it is sacred. Write a new stylesheet rather than editing this one, and decide afresh what furniture the page carries — the facts those passes established (below) are fixed; their form is not.

Component usage across the 166 slices, so you design for what is actually there (from `grep -l` over `notes-data/topics/`): `key-definition` on 157 pages, `diagram-figure` 94, `concept-table` 83, `formula-box` 29, `worked-example` 28, `calculation-table` 17, `exam-tip` 11, `content-example` 5, `evaluation-point` 2, `flow-chain` 1, `comp-spectrum` 1, `application` 0. `<h3>` on 113 pages. Sections per page run from 1 (four pages) to 16.

`revision-notes-textbook.css` is also loaded by the two diagram galleries and `revision-notes/macro-application/` (`verify_css_load_order.py` names them). Those three are not part of this redesign; either they must render as they do today, or you include them deliberately and say so. The seven hubs load `revision-notes-topics.css`, were redesigned on 23 August and are out of scope.

## The absolute rule: the economics content is identical

Every heading, paragraph, list, definition, table, formula, worked example, exam tip, diagram (same image file, same `alt`, same `width`/`height`, same caption) and specification-coverage sentence stays byte-for-byte what it is now, in the same order. "Import the content into the new design" means exactly that: the words, numbers, symbols, heading levels, `alt` texts and the sequence of the teaching sections are carried over unchanged, and everything about how they are presented is yours. The one positional exception you may propose is the `spec-alert`, which reads as page furniture as much as content — re-homing it at build time, the way the diagram-gallery line already is, is allowed if the design wants it elsewhere. Anything that would need new economics wording — a summary, "key takeaways", an "in one sentence" line — may be designed as a slot but not filled; I write those, or they are derived verbatim from text already on the page. This is CLAUDE.md hard rule 2 and it is not negotiable.

Two consequences for how you build it:

- **Structure goes in the generator, not the slices.** Any wrapper, attribute, id or landmark the new design needs on content goes in at build time, the way `notes_extras.py` already adds `<h2>` ids and `build_notes_pages.py` wraps diagrams in `<picture>`. Measure every anchor you splice against across all 166 slices before relying on it (the standard set in `notes_extras.py`'s docstring), and fail the build loudly on a miss. If the design genuinely needs a change inside the slices themselves, it goes in the Gate 2 plan as a separate, named proposal with its reason and its verification — I decide, not you. Default is no.
- **Prove it, don't assert it.** For the mock: strip tags from the content region of your mock and of the live generated page and diff — zero differences in text, and the same image `src`/`alt` list in the same order. For the roll-out: `python3 scripts/verify_text_integrity.py <base>` reports zero removed or altered wording across all 166 (additions are chrome only), and `verify_markup_integrity.py <base> --strict` with `Markup-Change:` declared per its rules.

## Editing must stay easy

I add economics content to these pages by hand, in VSCode, and will keep doing so — a paragraph, a definition, a worked example, a diagram, sometimes a whole section. Today that means editing the slice in `notes-data/topics/<dir>/<slug>.html`, running `python3 scripts/build.py`, and hoping the anchors the generator splices against still match. The redesign must make that easier, not harder:

- **The source of truth stays one plain HTML file per topic** in `notes-data/topics/`, editable with nothing beyond VSCode. Do not move content into JSON, split it across files, or add per-page configuration the design needs to work. If you believe a different source format (Markdown, say) would genuinely serve me better, make the case at Gate 2 with the risk stated honestly — the repo moved off a markdown-to-page path after a scripted rebuild silently destroyed `<a>` tags (CLAUDE.md hard rule 6), and tables, formulae and worked examples need HTML anyway. Default is HTML slices.
- **Every build-time transform tolerates hand-written HTML.** An anchor a reasonable hand edit could break — exact indentation, a bare tag, a specific line — is a trap. Prefer anchors that survive reformatting and honest mistakes; where you must be strict, fail the build with a message naming the file, the line and what to change. Never a silent skip, never a stack trace. Test it: take a scratch copy of a slice, add a paragraph, a `key-definition`, a diagram figure and a worked example the way I would, and confirm the build either renders them correctly or tells me plainly why not.
- **Adding a component is copy-paste.** Every component in the new design has one canonical markup snippet that works when pasted into a slice with no further wiring — no ids to invent, no numbering to maintain, no attribute the generator needs me to remember. Heading ids, figure numbering if you keep it, WebP twins, contents entries and anything else derived stay derived at build time.
- **Write the guide.** `docs/EDITING-NOTES.md`, in plain English for me, not for Claude: which file to open for a given URL; the skeleton of a slice and what must not be touched; a copy-paste snippet for every component with one line on when to use it; how to add a diagram (PNG into `images/diagrams/`, `python3 scripts/build_diagram_webp.py --apply`, the figure snippet with `width`, `height` and `alt`); how to add a formula; then `python3 scripts/build.py`, check in Live Server, commit with the `Text-Change:` trailer `suggest_trailers.py` prints. Point at `scripts/new_topic.py` for a new topic. Two pages at most. Update `notes-data/CLAUDE.md` and the component table in `revision-notes/CLAUDE.md` to match — those are for Claude; the guide is for me.

- **Fonts are self-hosted, and there are two families:** Source Sans Pro (in `main.css`, six cuts) and Merriweather (in `revision-notes-textbook.css`, three). ## Creative freedom — what I actually want you to think about

Start with a short design audit of the current page at 1280 and 360 (what is dated, what fights the content, what is wasted vertical space, where the eye goes), then put it aside and design from a blank sheet: what does the best A-Level revision-notes page on the web look like for a student on a phone the night before a mock, and for Google deciding whether this page or Save My Exams' answers "demand curve shift"? Directions worth considering — pick what earns its place, don't do all of them, and say what you rejected and why:

- **A reading layout, not a card.** A measured reading column (roughly 65–75 characters) for the prose, with the contents list becoming a sticky "On this page" rail on wide screens (≥1024px or so) that tracks the current section, and staying the in-flow list it is today below that. Diagrams and wide tables can break out of the column.
- **A calmer, more coherent system.** One typographic scale, one palette, tokens in `:root` (that is where this sheet's tokens already live). The callout boxes and inline chips should read as one family with clear hierarchy — definitions, exam tips and worked examples are the things a student scans for, so make them scannable — rather than seven competing treatments with gradients and badges. Decide whether the floating pill labels survive; if they do, fix their contrast.
- **Definitions.** 157 pages carry `key-definition` chips inline in a paragraph. Make them look like definitions a student would revise from. The glossary is extracted from these chips (`glossary-data/CLAUDE.md`) — the class name and the chip's text stay exactly as they are.
- **Diagrams.** 94 pages carry them and they are the thing students screenshot. Consider a larger default size in the reading column, a tap-to-enlarge on phones (an `<a href>` to the PNG works with no JS; a `<dialog>` is a fine enhancement), and whether the first diagram above the fold should carry `fetchpriority="high"` — a build-time attribute, like the existing `loading="lazy"` on later ones. `docs/DIAGRAM_STYLE.md` is the locked style of the images themselves; they do not change.
- **Tables on phones.** The current answer is a horizontal scroll with a "↔ Scroll to view full table" strip. Decide whether that is the right answer for 83 pages of comparison tables at 360px, or whether there is a better one that keeps the markup.
- **Formulae.** 29 pages render `formula-box` via MathJax from a CDN (the KaTeX migration in `OWNER-TODO.md` "Ideas" is out of scope here — design for MathJax output as it is, and note if the design would benefit from the migration).
- **Reasons to come back.** These are the features that turn a one-visit page into a revision tool, and none of them should cost a reader who has JS off anything: a reading-progress indicator; the `timeRequired` already in each page's JSON-LD surfaced as "N min read" beside the update date; a "Key terms on this page" strip derived at build time from the page's own `key-definition` chips (existing wording, no new economics — but note many chips read "Definition:" rather than naming the term, so look at how `scripts/extract_glossary.py` resolves a term before relying on chip text); a per-topic "Mark as revised" tick stored in `localStorage` in the same idiom as the flashcards' "progress saved on this device", so a student can see what they have covered across a theme; a back-to-top control on long pages; a print layout that gives a clean A4 handout (the site-wide print block landed on 24 August — build on it, don't fight it). A dark mode is allowed only if it is complete — the diagrams are opaque PNGs on white and would need a treatment — and cheap; if it is neither, say so and skip it.
- **Performance is part of the design.** The sheet is 46 KB and the page is ~38 KB of HTML before images; measure what your version costs. No layout shift as fonts or chrome arrive (the metric-matched fallbacks exist for this — keep using them), a stable LCP element, and nothing render-blocking that was not already.

On SEO, be honest about what a design can and cannot do. The levers that matter most for these pages — topic-first titles, schema, author, internal links, twin links — are done. What is in the design's gift: Core Web Vitals (CLS and LCP above all), all text and the board-specific chrome visible on first paint, an intact `h1` → `h2` → `h3` outline with the existing stable ids, image dimensions and `alt` text kept, less CSS, a page that mobile-first indexing sees whole. Do not add structured data the visible page does not support, and do not add copy for its own sake. Design for the student; the ranking case follows from the page being better to read.
