Factors of Production

Specification Coverage: Edexcel B unit 1.2.3 - Factors of Production. Students need to explain enterprise and the other three factors, capital, land and labour, through the resources a business actually uses: premises, equipment, raw materials, managers and employees.

What the Factors of Production Are

Factors of production: the resources used to make goods and provide services. Economists group them into four: land, labour, capital and enterprise.

Every business, from a window cleaner to a car maker, uses all four in some mix. What changes is the balance. A software firm needs skilled staff and computers but very little land. A farm needs a lot of land and machinery but only a few workers. All four factors are scarce (unit 1.1.1), so each one has a price the business must pay to use it.

Enterprise

Enterprise: the willingness and ability to organise the other factors of production and to take the risk of running a business.

Enterprise is the factor that sets the others to work. The entrepreneur (unit 1.2.1) chooses what to produce, decides how much land, labour and capital to use and in what combination, and puts money at risk. The reward for enterprise is profit, and unlike the other rewards it is not agreed in advance. If the business does badly there may be no profit at all.

Capital, Land and Labour in a Business

Land: natural resources used in production, such as water, crops, timber and oil. For a business, land also includes its premises.

Labour: the physical and mental effort people put into production.

Capital: man-made resources used in production, such as machinery, tools and vehicles.

The spec lists five things a business uses. The table places each one against its factor, using a high-street café as the example.

Factor What the business uses In a café Reward
Land Its premises and raw materials The shop itself; coffee beans, milk, water Rent
Labour Managers and employees The manager, baristas and kitchen staff Wages and salaries
Capital Equipment Coffee machine, ovens, fridges, tills Interest
Enterprise The owners or founders, who set up the business and take the risk The person who opened the café Profit

Premises, the shop, office or factory a business works from, count as land. So do raw materials, because they start as natural resources: the coffee beans a café buys began as a crop.

The quality of a factor matters as well as the amount. Staff who are trained and experienced produce more per hour, and newer equipment breaks down less often. Spending on training and up-to-date machinery is how a business gets more output from the same number of workers.

Owners are enterprise; managers and workers are labour. The owners or founders set up the business and risk their own money, so their reward is profit. Managers and workers are paid a wage or salary whether the business does well or badly.
