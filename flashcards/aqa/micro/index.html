Why is economics a social science, and how does its methodology differ from the natural sciences?

Economics applies the scientific method — observation, hypothesis formation, testing, refinement — to human behaviour and societies.

Unlike the natural sciences it cannot run controlled laboratory experiments, so economists build models resting on simplifying assumptions and test them against real-world data.

Different assumptions can lead economists to different conclusions from the same data.

State the formula for price elasticity of demand (PED), and how to interpret its value.

PED=%ΔQD%ΔP\text{PED} = \frac{\%\Delta Q_D}{\%\Delta P}PED=%ΔP%ΔQD​​

PED is negative for a normal demand curve — classify on the magnitude:

  • 0 = perfectly inelastic.
  • Between 0 and 1 = relatively inelastic.
  • 1 = unitary.
  • Above 1 = relatively elastic.
  • Infinity = perfectly elastic.

Interpret the points on a production possibility diagram: on, inside and outside the frontier.

  • Points on the frontier (A, C, D, B) are productively efficient — all resources fully and efficiently used.
  • Point E inside the frontier means unemployed or inefficiently used resources: more of both goods is possible at zero opportunity cost.
  • Point F outside is currently unattainable with existing resources and technology.

A movement along the frontier, such as C to D, shows the opportunity cost of producing more consumer goods.
