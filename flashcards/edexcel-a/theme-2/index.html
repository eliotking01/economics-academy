Sample cards

Define GDP, and distinguish actual from potential growth.

Gross Domestic Product — the total monetary value of all final goods and services produced within a country's borders in a given year: the primary measure of economic activity.

Actual growth is a rise in real GDP — producing more than last year. Potential growth is a rise in productive capacity — what the economy could produce at full employment.

State the expenditure approach to measuring GDP.

GDP=C+I+G+(X−M)GDP = C + I + G + (X - M)GDP=C+I+G+(X−M)

Consumption + Investment + Government spending + Net exports. The income approach — summing wages, rent, interest and profit — must reach the same total, because every pound spent is a pound of income for someone.

Show demand-pull inflation on an AD/AS diagram.

Aggregate demand shifts right (AD1 to AD2) — from rising consumer spending, investment, government spending or net exports — while SRAS is unchanged. The equilibrium moves up the SRAS curve: the price level rises from PL1 to PL2 as output rises from Y1 to Y2.

Prices are pulled up by excess demand, which is why demand-pull inflation strengthens as the economy nears full capacity.
