Define globalisation — and what characterises it?

Globalisation: the increasing integration and interdependence of the world's economies.

Its characteristics: increased free trade in goods and services across borders; increased foreign direct investment (FDI) and capital flows; increased foreign ownership of firms through multinational and transnational corporations; and increased freedom of movement of labour between countries.

Country A can make 40m cars or 80m tonnes of wheat; Country B can make 10m cars or 60m tonnes of wheat. Calculate each opportunity cost — who should specialise in what?

Opportunity cost of a car:

  • A gives up 80 ÷ 40 = 2 tonnes.
  • B gives up 60 ÷ 10 = 6 tonnes.

Opportunity cost of a tonne of wheat:

  • A gives up 0.5 cars.
  • B gives up 10 ÷ 60 ≈ 0.17 cars.

A has the lower opportunity cost in cars, B in wheat — so A specialises in cars and B in wheat, even though A has an absolute advantage in both.

Show comparative advantage using two countries' PPFs.

Germany's PPF runs from 150mn T-shirts to 20mn chips; Vietnam's from 200mn T-shirts to 10mn chips — each has an absolute advantage in one good.

Opportunity costs:

  • A chip costs Germany 7.5 T-shirts but Vietnam 20.
  • A T-shirt costs Vietnam 0.05 chips but Germany 0.13.

Each specialises where its opportunity cost is lower: fully specialised, they produce 20m chips and 200mn T-shirts — beating the 15m and 175mn made without specialisation — and by trading, both consume beyond their own PPFs.
